SonicWall (TZ/NSa & SonicOS) to Cato

The classic UK mid-market SonicWall estate is TZ desktop firewalls at every branch, an NSa at HQ, NSM (or retired GMS) in the middle, and SSL-VPN remote access terminating on the firewalls themselves — almost always bought and run through an MSP. That last part is the story: firewall-terminated SSL-VPN was the reported entry point in two Akira ransomware waves, and the MySonicWall cloud-backup breach means the estate's configurations and credentials must be treated as adversary-held. This playbook maps the whole network stack to the Cato SASE Cloud — SSL-VPN cohorts to ZTNA first, branch waves to Sockets behind them — and rebuilds policy clean on Cato rather than re-importing compromised configuration.

Business objective

Why this matters — and why now

This is the most incident-driven displacement in the library. A Gen 6 SonicWall estate in 2026 is at or past end of support (Gen 6 entered Limited Retirement Mode on 20 April 2024 — critical fixes only), its legacy management platform GMS hit its last order day on 1 October 2025, and its dedicated remote-access line — the SMA 100 — was force-retired on 31 October 2025 with barely two months' runway and no-charge transition offers. Meanwhile the SSL-VPN service running on the firewalls themselves was the initial-access vector incident responders reported across the 2024 and 2025 ransomware campaigns, and in September 2025 SonicWall disclosed that an unauthorised party had accessed firewall configuration backups in MySonicWall cloud backup — an investigation concluded with Mandiant on 8 October 2025, confirming every customer who used the cloud backup service was affected.

SonicWall's proposed fix is another appliance generation — Gen 8 hardware shipping from 2025, with Secure Upgrade Plus trade-ins pushed at every end-of-support notice — plus a bolted-on SSE (Cloud Secure Edge, the former Banyan). The refresh money is being spent either way; the honest question is whether it buys a third appliance cycle or removes the appliance cycle. The objective: replace the TZ/NSa estate, the firewall-to-firewall VPN mesh and the firewall-terminated SSL-VPN with Cato — the exploited surface first — and rebuild policy clean on the platform rather than re-importing configuration that must be presumed compromised.

What SonicWall itself confirmed primary advisories

CVE-2024-40766 (SNWLID-2024-0015, CVSS 9.3, published 22 Aug 2024): improper access control in SonicOS management access and SSL-VPN across Gen 5, 6 and 7; flagged for potential active exploitation in early September 2024 and added to CISA's KEV catalogue on 9 Sep 2024 — with explicit guidance to reset all local SSL-VPN passwords, especially on configs imported from Gen 6 to Gen 7. Aug 2025 Gen 7 campaign (notice 4 Aug, updated 22 Aug 2025): SonicWall concluded with "high confidence" it was not a zero-day but correlated with CVE-2024-40766 — fewer than 40 incidents, many tied to Gen 6 → Gen 7 migrations where local passwords were carried over and never reset; guidance was SonicOS 7.3 (July 2025, with added brute-force and MFA protections) plus another full password reset. NHS England issued cyber alert CC-4686 on this activity. MySonicWall breach (17 Sep 2025): config backup files accessed; on 8 Oct 2025 SonicWall and Mandiant confirmed all cloud-backup customers were affected — the files hold encrypted credentials and full configuration, and SonicWall's own notice says possession "could increase the risk of targeted attacks". Remediation is an "Essential Credential Reset" across local users, TOTP bindings and IPsec keys.

What third parties reported attribute as reporting

Arctic Wolf reported roughly 30 Akira and Fog ransomware intrusions from August 2024 with initial access via SonicWall SSL-VPN accounts on devices unpatched for CVE-2024-40766 — logins typically from VPS infrastructure, encryption often same-day. Macnica's December 2024 leak-site analysis put about half of the organisations on Akira/Fog leak sites on SonicWall, with ~48,933 devices still exposed and unpatched. Arctic Wolf then reported the campaign resuming from 22 Jul 2025 against patched Gen 7 firewalls with SSL-VPN enabled — largely credentials carried over from Gen 6 imports — and The Hacker News/Rapid7 reported malicious logins that passed OTP MFA challenges (consistent with seeds harvested earlier), with encryption in under four hours; CISA/FBI reporting put Akira proceeds at ~$244M by late September 2025. Google Threat Intelligence separately documented UNC6148 planting the OVERSTEP backdoor on fully patched, end-of-life SMA 100 appliances in July 2025. The Hacker News reported in November 2025 that SonicWall attributed the backup breach to a state-sponsored actor — SonicWall's own notice says only "unauthorized party".

Treat the configuration as compromised — rebuild, don't re-import

Two structural lessons set the migration design. First, SSL-VPN terminating on the edge firewall is the single most exploited entry point in this estate class — ZTNA from a cloud PoP removes that surface entirely, so the SSL-VPN cohorts migrate first. Second, the 2025 wave's mechanic was configuration carry-over: old local passwords imported into new boxes. The same logic condemns any config re-import into the new platform — and after the MySonicWall breach, every secret in the estate (local VPN accounts, IPsec PSKs, SNMP strings, admin credentials) is potentially adversary-held regardless of patch level. This migration is review-and-map with full secret rotation, never lift-and-shift.

Questions worth asking

“How many of your firewalls are still Gen 6 — and who is patching them now they're in Limited Retirement Mode? Did you use MySonicWall cloud backup, and has the Essential Credential Reset actually been completed on every box? And where did your SMA 100 users land after 31 October 2025?”

The shape of the play

A full network-stack displacement — bring the MSP with you

Concede honestly; it builds credibility. TZ boxes are cheap, familiar to UK MSPs, and bundle a lot for the money — gateway anti-malware, IPS, App Control, content filtering and anti-spam in the Gen 7 Essential Protection suite. SonicWall is a 100% channel vendor with 17,000+ partners, claiming roughly 500,000 business customers in 215 countries in its own boilerplate — and Capture ATP with RTDMI was genuinely differentiated SMB sandboxing when it launched, backed by real threat research in Capture Labs. Unlike a Sophos displacement, though, there is no beloved endpoint suite to preserve here: this is a full network-stack displacement. What you must bring along is the MSP relationship and the operational muscle memory — zone thinking and per-box rules become one global policy; the story is “same intent, one rulebase, no boxes to patch”.

“SonicWall has SASE too” — Cloud Secure Edge

Concede CSE exists — SonicWall acquired Banyan Security (announced 3 Jan 2024, completed 26 Dec 2023) and sells it as Cloud Secure Edge. The counters: it is an SSE bolted beside the firewall estate — the appliances, their firmware cycle and their refresh economics all remain; there is no private backbone; and most customers met CSE as the forced landing zone of the SMA 100 shutdown, not as a chosen platform. Cato replaces the appliance layer rather than supplementing it.

Use the security history architecturally

This is not “SonicWall is uniquely bad”. SonicWall published advisories promptly, shipped SonicOS 7.3 hardening, gave SMA users free transition licences, and brought in Mandiant. The argument is that the estate's crown jewels — VPN credentials, PSKs, full configs — live on and around WAN-facing appliances and the vendor's cloud, and both were compromised classes in 2024–2025. The same KEV-class story exists for Fortinet, Cisco and Ivanti; a cloud-delivered PoP removes the category, not the vendor.

The MSP is the real buyer

100% channel: the box margin and the management fee are the partner's, and the MSP relationship usually outlasts the hardware — in MSSP-run estates the firewalls are managed from the MSP's NSM tenant. A rip-and-replace pitched to the end customer that strands the MSP's margin will be resisted regardless of technical merit. Bring the MSP into the deal on Cato's partner-delivered model: they keep the customer, swap box-patching for platform operations, and their NSM inventory becomes the discovery source for the project.

The Cato solution

Recommended migration path

Provenance — read before you present

This page is authored from SonicWall's primary advisories, vendor documentation and Cato PS methodology research; it is not a delivery-verified runbook. Review the design — especially the on-ramp, route filtering and rollback details, and anything contract-dependent — with Cato Professional Services before committing it to a customer plan.

The arc follows the Cato PS methodology — Export → Review & Map → Deploy → Optimise, run as discovery → co-existence → pilot → phased cutover → decommission. TZ-heavy branch estates use two co-existence patterns, often combined: a parallel Socket per site (X1500/X1600 alongside the TZ, L3 handoff, subnets moved progressively — rollback is re-plugging) and an IPsec IKEv2 on-ramp, where remaining SonicWalls tunnel into the nearest Cato PoP as interim sites — for sites awaiting Socket delivery, short-lease sites, or as the hub interconnect carrying un-migrated branch traffic to Cato-side resources during the transition. The SonicWall S2S VPN stays authoritative for un-migrated sites, with default routes filtered at the on-ramp. The one deliberate inversion from other playbooks: remote access does not wait for the site waves — the SSL-VPN cohorts move to ZTNA early, because that is the exploited surface.

Migrated — Sockets and ZTNA cohorts via the nearest PoP Legacy / interim — SonicWall S2S VPN and IPsec on-ramp Attack path — removed with the SSL-VPN portal
Interim co-existence for a TZ-heavy estate: the SSL-VPN cohorts move to ZTNA first, retiring the exploited WAN-facing portal; TZ branches swap to parallel Sockets in waves sequenced by the end-of-support calendar; and the NSa hub runs an interim IPsec IKEv2 on-ramp carrying un-migrated branch traffic to Cato-side resources, with default routes filtered. Rollback at any point is re-plugging the TZ — its site-to-site VPN re-establishes to the hub.

  1. Phase 0 — Discovery and export (NSM is the discovery source)

    Inventory from the NSM device inventory export (CSV/JSON — one pass over the whole estate; in MSSP-run estates this lives in the MSP's tenant): models and generations per site (any Gen 6 is at or past end of support — lead with the calendar above), SonicOS versions (anything pre-7.3 on Gen 7 signals patch-cadence risk), the bundle per box from MySonicWall licensing (CGSS/AGSS vs EPSS/APSS — what is actually enforced), renewal dates, NSM vs GMS vs unmanaged, MSP contract terms, SMA history (where did those users land after 31 Oct 2025?), SonicWave/Switch dependencies, and USB LTE failover per TZ — confirm the Cato-side equivalent per site design now, not on cutover night. Export each firewall three ways: the .exp settings export (rollback/reference — not human-readable), the Tech Support Report (plain-text running config — the mapping-workbook input, exportable centrally via NSM), and the SonicOS API (JSON objects, rules and NAT policies — the machine-readable feed for bulk mapping). Capture per-access-rule Rx/Tx statistics to identify dead rules (clear counters, re-read after 30 days) and AppFlow/Analytics baselines for the app-category mapping. Finally the secrets audit — non-negotiable: enumerate local users (especially SSL-VPN-enabled), IPsec PSKs, SNMP strings and admin accounts. Given the backup breach and the password-carry-over history, everything gets rotated at migration and local VPN accounts are never recreated on the Cato side — identity comes from the IdP.

  2. Phase 1 — Co-existence foundation

    Stand up the Cato account: CMA admin model, IdP SSO and SCIM against Entra ID (the UK mid-market default), base policy from Cato best-practice templates. Deploy the two patterns: parallel Sockets (X1500/X1600) at the first-wave TZ sites, and IPsec IKEv2 on-ramps from retained SonicWalls — including the NSa hub interconnect that carries un-migrated branch traffic to Cato-side resources. Keep the SonicWall S2S VPN authoritative for un-migrated sites and filter default routes at the on-ramp. Sequence the waves by the EOS calendar: the 16 Apr 2026 models and the Aug 2026 TZ400/600 wave first — those boxes must be replaced with something regardless.

  3. Phase 2 — Pilot (map validated, deploy monitor-first)

    Security → Internet Firewall Security → TLS Inspection

    Pick 1–3 representative sites: one TZ branch plus the HQ NSa with the densest rule base. Map zone-matrix rules to Cato WAN/Internet Firewall; CFS profiles to URL categories plus custom categories; App Rules to app-category rules; DPI-SSL exclusions to TLS inspection bypass rules; Capture ATP scoping to Anti-Malware settings. Review-and-map, not lift-and-shift: per-box rule bases collapse into one global policy and dead rules (hit counters, Phase 0) are dropped, not migrated. Run IPS, Anti-Malware and new block rules in monitor/log first; enable TLS inspection on a pilot group after swapping the DPI-SSL resigning CA for the Cato certificate in MDM. Pilot users run the Cato Client alongside the NetExtender/GVC cohorts — validate SSO, MFA at the IdP, posture checks, and everything that lived as a Virtual Office bookmark (clientless browser access).

  4. Phase 3 — Phased site cutover

    Network → Sites

    TZ sites by wave: Socket in parallel → move VLANs → remove the site from the SonicWall VPN topology → validate → leave the TZ racked through the soak window. Rollback is re-enabling the tunnel. Inventory and re-create inbound NAT/port-forwards per site before cutover — branch servers, CCTV, third-party maintenance access — because Cato-side equivalents (remote port forwarding, allocated IPs) are a different model; surface this in discovery, not on cutover night. Update anything keyed to site egress IPs (SaaS allowlists, partner firewalls) per wave.

  5. Phase 4 — Remote access displacement (the de-risking moment)

    Access → Client Connectivity Policy Access → Device Posture

    Cohort rollout of the Cato Client via MDM; NetExtender and GVC profiles removed per cohort. Disable the SSL-VPN service and Virtual Office portal on each firewall as its last cohort clears — this is the measurable de-risking moment; log it as evidence. Never migrate firewall-local VPN accounts: all Cato access is IdP-backed with MFA and device posture. SMA-refugee users already on CSE move in the same cohorts.

  6. Phase 5 — Optimise and decommission

    Tighten monitor-mode rules to block, widen TLS inspection scope, tune QoS; use CMA analytics to retire never-hit rules quarter by quarter. Decommission in order: branch TZs → hub NSa and its on-ramp → any SSL-VPN portals not already dark → lapse the EPSS/APSS/CGSS renewals; cancel or repurpose NSM (keep it short-term if it still manages SonicWave APs or Switches); and delete the MySonicWall cloud backups once appliances are retired — they remain an exfiltration target even post-migration.

🧯

The exploited surface goes first

WAN-facing SSL-VPN portals at project start versus zero at finish is the headline metric — the entry point in every campaign above leaves the estate before the site waves even finish.

🔑

Secrets leave the edge

Local VPN accounts are never recreated; PSKs are new and rotated; identity comes from the IdP with MFA and posture. The rotation log is auditable against SonicWall's own Essential Credential Reset guidance.

🔁

Rollback is designed in

Re-plug the TZ and its site-to-site VPN re-establishes to the hub; re-enable a NetExtender profile per cohort. No rebuilds mid-window, and the TZ stays racked through the soak.

Sequence waves by the EOS calendar

The 16 Apr 2026 models (SOHO/SOHOW/TZ 300P/NSa 9250–9650) and the 1 Aug 2026 wave (TZ400/TZ600/NSA 3600/3650) must be replaced with something regardless — put them in the first Socket waves and the refresh budget converts directly. Gen 7 boxes with runway left migrate later, and their EPSS/APSS renewals simply lapse per wave.

Log the de-risking as you go

Evidence to collect per wave: Gen 6 boxes past end of support at project start (NSM inventory export); WAN-facing SSL-VPN portals at start versus zero at finish; rule-base collapse (per-box rule counts summed vs the final global policy, with the percentage of dead rules identified by hit counters); secrets rotated — local accounts removed and PSKs retired, auditable against the MySonicWall remediation guidance; and firmware/emergency-advisory patch events no longer applicable per site per year.

Gotchas & objections

What will come up in the room

“We already patched CVE-2024-40766”

The 2025 wave hit patched Gen 7 boxes — SonicWall's own notice ties many incidents to imported credentials that were never reset, not to the bug. And the cloud-backup breach means config secrets may be adversary-held regardless of patch level. The exposure is the architecture — WAN-facing SSL-VPN plus secrets bound to appliances — not one CVE. Use the advisory citations verbatim; never embellish.

“We'll just move to CSE”

CSE (the acquired Banyan) is an SSE beside the firewall estate: the TZ/NSa boxes, their firmware cycle and the Gen 8 refresh all remain, and there is no private backbone. Customers met it as the forced landing zone of the SMA 100 shutdown. If the customer is paying refresh money anyway, the comparison is appliance cycle + bolted-on SSE versus removing the appliance cycle.

The anti-spam gap

EPSS bundles Comprehensive Anti-Spam; Cato does not do email security. It is nearly always redundant with M365/Google email hygiene — but verify MX records and any on-box smart-host arrangements before promising parity, and price the replacement if one is genuinely needed.

SonicWave APs and SonicWall Switches

They are managed from the firewall or NSM — removing the firewall orphans their management. Inventory them in Phase 0, keep NSM running for LAN gear short-term, or fold a LAN refresh into the project. Do not let this surface on cutover night.

Branch inbound NAT

TZ estates accumulate port-forwards — local servers, CCTV, BMS, third-party maintenance access. The Cato model (remote port forwarding, allocated IPs) is different, so inventory every DNAT in discovery and move published services on their own change windows.

DPI-SSL practicalities cut both ways

Many SonicWall estates never fully enabled DPI-SSL — per-model connection limits and CA-deployment pain saw to that. That means the Cato TLS inspection rollout may be the customer's first full TLS inspection: plan exclusions (banking, health) and user comms deliberately rather than assuming like-for-like.

Don't overclaim — and be fair

Reseller notices say SMA 100 devices “cease to function” after 31 Oct 2025; SonicWall's own wording is end-of-support plus no-charge migration. Say “force-retired with two months' runway and no-charge migration offers” — fully supported — rather than “bricked”, unless the customer's own devices confirm it. More broadly: SonicWall published advisories promptly, shipped SonicOS 7.3 hardening, and brought in Mandiant. The argument is the appliance category and the secrets-on-the-edge model — the same story as Fortinet, Cisco and Ivanti — not vendor character assassination. The customer chose SonicWall; respect the choice and displace the architecture.
